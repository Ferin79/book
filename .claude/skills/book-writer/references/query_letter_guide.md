# Query Letter & Publishing Toolkit — Memoir Edition

**Purpose:** To generate publishing-ready assets from the completed memory bank. All the information needed for these documents already lives in the project's memory files — this guide shows how to assemble them for a true-story/memoir project.

Use after the manuscript (or a substantial portion of it) is complete and revised.

---

## Back-Cover Blurb (150–200 words)

**Formula:** Hook → Real Person + World → Stakes → Question

**Template:**

```
[HOOK — 1 striking sentence that captures the real emotional premise]

[SETUP — Who is the father/central figure? What was his real world? What did he want? 1-2 sentences]

[TURNING POINT — What real event disrupted things? What was genuinely at stake? 1-2 sentences]

[STAKES QUESTION — End with an implicit or explicit question that the reader can only answer by reading]
```

**Data to pull from memory bank:**
- `Core/story_structure.md` → Premise and central dramatic/emotional question
- `Core/people_and_places.md` → The father's/central figure's real wants and struggles
- `Core/story_structure.md` → Narrative arc and North Star

## 1-Page Synopsis (500–700 words)

A synopsis tells the full story including how it ends. Agents and publishers require this even for memoir.

**Structure:**

1. **Opening** (1 paragraph): The father/central figure, real setting, what he wanted, the real event that sets the story in motion
2. **Rising Circumstances** (2–3 paragraphs): Key real complications, escalating stakes, turning points, key relationships
3. **Hardest Point** (1 paragraph): The lowest or most difficult real moment and what was lost or nearly lost
4. **Resolution** (1 paragraph): What actually happened, how it resolved (or where the book chooses to end), and how the family/author's understanding changed

**Tone rules:**
- Write in present tense, third person (standard synopsis convention, even for a first-person book)
- Focus on cause-and-effect, not scene-by-scene description
- Do not use cliffhangers — reveal the real ending
- Each person introduced is named in ALL CAPS on first mention (e.g., **RAMESH**)
- State plainly that this is a true story — do not use fiction-marketing language ("a gripping thriller") that misrepresents the book's genre

**Data to pull from memory bank:**
- `Core/story_structure.md` → Full narrative arc
- `Outlines/Master_Outline.md` → Sequence of real events
- `Core/people_and_places.md` → People and relationships

## Query Letter (250–350 words)

**Structure:**

```
[Opening hook — 1 sentence that captures the book's real emotional core]

[Pitch paragraph — 2-3 sentences: the father/central figure, real world, real turning point, real stakes. This is the back-cover blurb compressed]

[Book details — 1 sentence: Title, category (memoir/narrative non-fiction), word count, comp titles if applicable]
Example: "[TITLE] is a [WORD COUNT]-word memoir in the tradition of [COMP TITLE 1] and [COMP TITLE 2]."

[Author bio — 1-2 sentences: your relationship to the subject and why you're the person to tell this story]

[Closing — thank the agent for their time, state what you're enclosing]
```

**Key rules:**
- Address the specific agent by name — never "Dear Agent"
- Do not pitch sequels or spin-offs
- Do not describe the book with fiction-marketing clichés ("a roller-coaster ride")
- Be explicit that this is a true story and that names/identifying details are used with the author's own authority (or note if any have been changed for privacy)

**Data to pull from memory bank:**
- `Core/projectbrief.md` → Category, word count, and book concept
- `Core/story_structure.md` → Pitch paragraph material

## Logline (25–30 words)

A logline distills the entire book into one sentence. Used for pitches, social media, and personal clarity.

**Formula:** `[Real person with a real want or circumstance] must [real pursuit] against [real obstacle/stakes] — and [what actually happened / what it cost].`

**Example:** *"A father who arrived in a new country with nothing must rebuild a life for his family from scratch — and the choices he made along the way outlast him."*

## Integration Notes for the AI
*When the author says "write a query letter", "generate a blurb", "write a synopsis", or "create a logline", read `Core/story_structure.md`, `Core/people_and_places.md`, and `Core/projectbrief.md` first, then follow the template for the requested format. Do not invent plot points, quotes, or outcomes not in the memory bank. Each output should feel like it was written by the author, not a generic AI — match the established tone from `Style/style_guide.md`, and always represent the book accurately as a true story.*
