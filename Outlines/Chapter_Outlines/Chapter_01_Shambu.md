# Chapter 1: Shambu

## Chapter Summary

The reader meets Rakesh Patel the way his own neighborhood met him: not by his given name, but by "Shambu," the nickname his street in Pal Gam, Surat gave him at birth. The chapter moves outward from that name — why he was called it, where and when he was born, the poor-to-lower-middle-class household he came from, the father (a farmer) and mother (a housewife) who couldn't help him toward an education, the law degree and English he earned anyway, his marriage to Heena Patel, the birth of his two children, the community's regard for him as honest and hardworking and devout, and his long, unrealized dream of settling in North America. That first movement closes on the street-level bookend: the name still fits, thirty years in.

**Extension (2026-08-17)**: A second movement follows, widening the lens from the street (Moti Fadiyu, all of it Kadva Patel Samaj, as is the rest of the village of Pal Gam) to the wider Kadva Patel Samaj across Gujarat. It shows him making sure his own children got the education he'd had to fight for (English medium school — a deliberate echo of his own self-taught English), then escalates his public standing: called on for weddings, funerals, and festivals, seen by the Samaj as the ideal man (no alcohol, no smoking, fully vegetarian, trustworthy). A further beat makes the relationship mutual rather than one-directional: he actively sought out Samaj involvement himself, uncritically, trusting that whatever the Samaj did was simply right for his own house too — framed, per the author's explicit instruction, as a character note about him, not as any criticism of the Samaj as an institution. The chapter then pivots, in its final lines, to the book's real subject: this standing wasn't free, something was being traded to maintain it, and the narrator — deliberately — does not say what. This is the opening "portrait" chapter of Part I — The Mask: establishing the public image in full, at both the street and Samaj scale, before later chapters complicate it.

## Key Beats (real, in order)

1. **Opening image**: the lane in Pal Gam calling out "Shambu" — establishes community, place, and the central puzzle of the name before explaining it.
2. **The name explained**: born Monday, 2 August 1976, the first Monday of Shravan month — the Gujarati calendar detail that gave him the name (Shambhu, a name of Shiva), which turned out to fit a boy who grew into serious, lifelong devotion.
3. **Where he came from**: born and raised in Pal Gam; father Sukhdevbhai Patel (farmer), mother Nandanben Patel (housewife); a childhood and teenage in poverty to lower-middle-class conditions.
4. **What he built anyway**: financed his own college education against that backdrop, earned a law degree, and separately learned English — both real achievements for his background and era. Acknowledge, rather than paper over, that the specific how of the financing isn't part of the family record.
5. **Family completed**: marries Heena Patel, 10 December 1998; son Ferin Patel born 9 December 1999; daughter Janvi Patel born 11 November 2002; stable Accounting job in Surat.
6. **Reputation**: known through the village/community for honesty, hard work, and devotion — prayer mornings and evenings, a committed follower of Lord Shiva. Tie back to the name.
7. **The wider dream**: a longstanding pull toward foreign countries and the idea of settling in North America; a Hong Kong work trip that made the idea concrete.
8. **The attempt**: IELTS exams at around age thirty; permanent-residency applications to the USA and Canada.
9. **The close**: it didn't happen. He stayed. He was happy with what he had anyway — closing image/line, restrained, no melodrama.
