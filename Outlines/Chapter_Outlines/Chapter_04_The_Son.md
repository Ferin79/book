# Chapter 4: The Son

*(Memoir outline. Every beat maps to the author's `plan-4.md` or to the author's direct answers to clarifying questions on 2026-10-11. Nothing is invented. Reconstructed dialogue follows the author's own notes.)*

## Chapter Summary

Opens Part IV (working name "The Father," see `CONTEXT.md`). It pays off the forward thread Chapter 3 planted: Rakesh Patel "never tried to be loved by his children. He tried to be obeyed by them," and how far that went "is its own chapter further on." The opening picks up Chapter 3's last image, his hands, and gives them "one fair page" first. The same hands paid for everything the narrator learned.

The chapter begins with honest gratitude: English medium school and afternoon tuitions, karate pushed hard (international tournaments in Sri Lanka and Nepal), Canada (his dream first), the master's in Halifax, and a life free of smoking and drinking. Then comes the turn: these were a father's basic duties done with a little extra effort, and paying for a child is not knowing him. From there the chapter follows one thread, choice, from childhood to marriage. First small choices are denied (TV, haircuts), then Sundays and fear (he hit the narrator many times until twelfth grade), then the endless smoking and drinking lectures (about image, not trust). The teenage control comes next: no *lafda*, no phone until after twelfth grade, no games, no night stays, approved clothes, trimmed beard, the 10 PM rule with ten calls. Then escape to Halifax in January 2022, and the control following by phone.

The second half is the marriage. The master's ends in June 2023 and five biodatas follow. Daily calls follow a pattern: polite, then losing the argument, then "Don't teach me. I'm your father." He uses foul language on son and mother, and beats Heena Patel to make her convince Ferin. Ferin gives up. Then comes the father's single filter, a rich house, and the Samaj's wealth-equals-status custom, which the author named directly for this chapter. The Vrunda call follows ("Am I marrying her family, or the girl?"). In the India trip in late 2023, Ferin chooses Vrunda from five viewings, and the father pushes richer families instead ("Hypocrite." / "That was the death of choice." / "I am ashamed to call him my father."). His mother, his Kaki, and his sister back him with reasons the father can't answer. He is engaged to Vrunda in December 2023. The chapter widens to the Samaj's disease and many fathers like his. It closes on the Taittiriya Upanishad's "follow our good conduct, not the rest." The narrator keeps the good and refuses the rest: "He spent my whole life choosing for me. This part, I choose."

## Author decisions for this chapter (2026-10-11)

- **Title**: "The Son" (continues Shambu → The Father → The Wife).
- **One chapter**, in great detail, not split in two.
- **Tone**: strongly worded. The author is writing his own feelings. The author's own strongest phrases are kept: "1800s," "narrow-minded," "I was living in hell," "Hypocrite," "the death of choice," "I am ashamed to call him my father," "the Samaj's disease," "cage."
- **The Samaj**: the Chapter 1 rule ("just a hint, don't blame the Samaj") is **lifted for this chapter only**. The Samaj's wealth-as-status custom is named directly. The main blame still rests on Rakesh Patel, who had the education and travel to break the chain.
- **Scripture**: the author asked for more verses, drawn from beyond the original five approved sources. Twelve verses come from seven distinct texts (see below).
- **Medical bills**: removed (author's instruction). Ch 3 says the doctor and medicines came from Heena's account.
- **Janvi**: kept brief here. Her rules were even stricter, and the **next chapter is hers alone** (author's instruction). This is planted in prose as a forward thread.

## POV

First person, Ferin Patel. Direct memory throughout. Janvi is mentioned only for the shared Sundays and fear, and the stricter rules are attributed to her as fact from the author.

## Setting

- **Location(s)**: the joint-family home on Moti Fali, Pal Gam, Surat (Sundays, lectures, teenage rules). Karate tournaments in Sri Lanka and Nepal (named only). Halifax, Canada (master's, then software job, phone calls). India again for a month at the end of 2023 (five girl viewings, engagement).
- **Time**: Ferin's childhood (2000s) through his engagement in December 2023. The reflective present is unpinned, with no present-day age (per the `people_and_places.md` note on Ch 2's "28").
- **Mood**: gratitude first, then a long tightening of control. Anger is stated plainly in the second half, and the chapter ends in resolve.

## Key Beats (in order)

1. **Bridge from Chapter 3**: "his hands" (what they did to his father and to the narrator's mother, and how he hid them) → "one fair page": the same hands paid for everything the narrator learned.
2. **Gratitude**: education was his one open hand, wider than school fees (reconciles with Ch 3). English medium school, afternoon tuitions, never cut back. Karate was 40% the narrator's choice and 60% his. Four or five belts up from white, international tournaments in Sri Lanka and Nepal, medals not remembered and not claimed. Canada was his dream first (Ch 1's IELTS and PR attempts), grew into the narrator's, and he supported the master's; now PR in Halifax. A clean life with no smoking or drinking (Ch 2 callback). [Scripture: **Chanakya Niti 2.11**: a parent who does not educate a child is an enemy. By that measure he was never the narrator's enemy. This is honest credit.] The turn: "only a father's basic duties... with a little extra effort." Paying for education is not raising a child, "and it is not the same as knowing him."
3. **No connection; no balance**: "My father never knew me." The usual Indian father-son relationship: the son is afraid. Strict about results ("What reason do you have for bad grades now?"). No balance between study and play, and no TV when he came home from the office. [Scripture: **Bhagavad Gita 6.17**: balance in food, recreation (*vihara*), work, and sleep. Krishna's own playful childhood in Gokul.] Haircuts he didn't approve. "He never liked anything." Thesis: never given an option. Small choices then, bigger ones later, and his hold never loosened.
4. **Sundays and fear**: handoff "That hold was tightest on Sundays." Most kids love Sunday, but it was his day off too: behave all day, no TV, nothing silly, a tense house. "We hated him on Sundays, and we hated Sundays too." Callback to Ch 3's money Sunday. He did not know how to be a father. Fear learned early from what he did to Sukhdev papa and to Heena, and it never left. [Scripture: **Bhagavad Gita 12.15**: dear to Krishna is the one from whom the world does not shrink. Two children shrank from him every Sunday.] **He hit the narrator** many times, at every age, until twelfth grade, with no reason worth giving, and kept hitting Heena. He never thought about child trauma: "He only knew how to be obeyed." "We still call him Papa... We don't have an option there either."
5. **The lectures**: "monthly sessions" (an admitted exaggeration). Don't smoke, don't drink, focus on studies, "Look at your grandfather. Look how he went because of it." The narrator was there the night Sukhdev papa died (Ch 2), and his father never mentioned the rod. The lesson was right, but the repetition felt like torture. Underneath: distrust and image, the Samaj's ideal son, when many boys in the Samaj were struggling in school.
6. **Teenage control**: handoff "The older I got, the harder he guarded that image." Different opinions against a "1800s," narrow-minded father. [Scripture: **Chanakya Niti 3.18**: pamper for five years, discipline for ten, treat as a friend at sixteen. Honest that *tadayet* means "strike." Even Chanakya set an end date, but the father's hand went past sixteen and the friendship never came.] No girlfriends, no *lafda*, for Samaj image, said by a man who travelled to ten countries and planned to settle abroad. [Scripture: **Kabir, Sakhi**: reading books doesn't make one wise, the two and a half letters of *prem* do.] The rules: no phone until after twelfth grade and nagging after, no games, no long talks with friends, no night-outs or night stays to study, approved clothes per event, beard and hair trimmed, home by ten or ten calls and anger. **Food (added 2026-10-12, author's addition)**: no pizza, burgers, Coke or other soft drinks, or bread items, because he dislikes them ("These things are poison"). The author agrees moderation is key, but a complete ban is wrong, especially in college. A callback to Gita 6.17's *yuktahara* (balanced eating, not banned eating), with no new verse block. Travel for a passport stamp and a Facebook post for the Samaj, not for other cultures and cuisines, and Italy as the counterexample. He never adapted to a new generation (Pal Gam into Surat, his children in English medium, a new generation's food and ideas), kept his ego big, and controlled the one place he could: "At home, there was no one he had to please." Restaurants: he and his brother Praful chose the place and the order for everyone, and "Everyone ate what he liked." "No freedom. No choice. No life." "I was living in hell." Friends' fathers were completely different. **Forward thread**: Janvi's rules were even stricter, and her story is the next chapter. "By the end of school, that house felt like a cage."
7. **Canada**: "I flew out of the cage." January 2022, master's in computer science, Halifax, age 22. "I was wrong." The smoking lectures at 22. "His morning was my night": why awake so late, what were you doing, why didn't you pick up. "The cage had simply come with me. Now it rang."
8. **The marriage pressure**: the master's ends in June 2023 and five biodatas follow. No preference asked, the right age in the caste, force. [Scripture: **Bhagavad Gita 18.63**: "Reflect on it fully, then do as you wish." God gave Arjuna a choice.] Daily calls: the polite opening ("Look at me. I got married at twenty-two."), Ferin's reasoned reply ("a completely different decade... Stop caring about the Samaj"), then "Don't teach me. I'm your father." [Scripture: **Yoga Vasishtha 2.18.3–4**: accept reason even from a child, and the man who drinks from his father's well beside the Ganga.] Foul language at son and mother, and **Heena beaten to make her convince Ferin** (author-confirmed). His mother was more modern than him and the Samaj. [Scripture: **Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva (Vidura Niti) 34.75**: the wound of words never heals.] Ferin gives up while working, with his mother beaten on the other side of the world: "I lost."
9. **The filter**: one filter, "a rich house." "Look where Rakesh got his son engaged." In the Samaj, well known means wealthy, and money is all that matters (named directly per the author). He could have broken the chain. [Scripture: **Bhagavad Gita 3.21**: whatever the leading man does, others follow. He was the Samaj's "ideal" man (Ch 1).] "A really good girl from a lower family is fine... But she is not suitable for our family." Backward "even in 2023."
10. **The Vrunda call**: a normal morning in Canada, during the software job. "There is a girl for you. Her name is Vrunda." Ten minutes on her family: well known, a mango export business, the elder sister married into a big family. "Am I marrying her family, or the girl?" [Scripture: **Katha Upanishad 1.1.4**: Nachiketa asks "Father, to whom will you give me?" Vajashravasa's showy gift of worn-out cows, and the joyless worlds of 1.1.3.]
11. **The India trip**: a couple of weeks of talking with Vrunda, then paused until the month-long trip. Made to talk with richer families' daughters in between. Five viewings were planned before he landed, and Vrunda was the only one who interested him. The father pushes the two better-known families. "Hypocrite." "That was the death of choice." "I am ashamed to call him my father." [Scripture: **Mahabharata, Aranyaka Parva 278.26**, Savitri: "I have chosen my husband once." Her father told her to choose by qualities (277.32). She chose the son of a blind, dethroned king living in a forest. Told he would die within a year, her father said "choose another" (278.23), and then accepted. Vat Savitri is observed across Gujarat.] "My father did not."
12. **Backup and engagement**: "this time, he was not the only voice in our house." His mother, his Kaki, and his sister stood with him, the two women Ch 3 showed as the house's unpaid servants, plus Janvi. Their logical arguments, and "For once in that house, reason won." Engaged to Vrunda in **December 2023**. She will be his wife soon. A good person of good character, "Not everyone is" lucky.
13. **The wider case** (the author asked for more here): many fathers in India force their children into families they like, and many such marriages fail. "I had about a week to choose." The Samaj's disease: a son's marriage is about what people will say, a girl is measured by her father's business, and it is handed down "as if it were dharma." Men who could change it choose not to, and his father tightened the chain. [Scripture: **Taittiriya Upanishad, Shiksha Valli 1.11.2**: treat your father as a god, and follow only our blameless deeds, "not the rest."]
14. **Close**: "So I am choosing." He keeps the education, the clean life, and the Canada dream. He refuses the fear, the control, the image lectures, and the Samaj's disease: "The chain he chose not to break ends with me." Last line: "He spent my whole life choosing for me. This part, I choose."
