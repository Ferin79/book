## Chapter Summary

The chapter continues Part IV ("The Father") and keeps the promise Chapter 4 made: "My sister's rules were even stricter than mine. Her story belongs to the next chapter." The author calls it the most important chapter of the book. It opens on Chapter 4's last word, *choose*: Ferin could choose what to keep from his father, while Janvi "had to fight just to leave his house."

The first half follows Janvi Patel from birth to leaving home at 19. She was born a week after Diwali and welcomed as Lakshmi, and the wealth came after her, a hint at Pal Gam joining Surat (kept indirect, per the plan). Her childhood was shared with Ferin, fear included, with no father-daughter bond. She was the quiet, brilliant child who never asked for anything. Image-driven doctor lectures began in high school. In eleventh and twelfth grade (biology) came constant nagging and tears. Her first NEET in 2020 was postponed twice by COVID, and she missed a general-category government seat by a few marks. Her father raged over his image and forced a drop year. That year she studied day and night, his calls doubled and his taunts grew, and she thought of ending her life. Her mother kept her safe. In 2021 she won a government-quota seat at Zydus Medical College, Dahod. Her father opposed it to keep control and pushed a third attempt. Janvi refused, and for the first time she raised her voice. He raised his hand at her, and her mother stopped him. Her mother and Ferin (from Halifax, by phone) backed her. She left at once. Her father posted on Facebook and took the credit. It closes on the plan's forward thread: "something even bigger was waiting for her."

Two image systems run through the half:
- **Lakshmi**: a Lakshmi "who will never leave" (Sri Sukta, opening) → "the Lakshmi he showed off in the Samaj" (raised hand) → all women are the Goddess (Devi Mahatmya) → "The Lakshmi of our house had left it" (Mahabharata 12.221: Lakshmi leaves a house of anger where children are not cared for).
- **Shade**: the father as Kabir's date palm (tall, no shade for the traveller) → the mother as the "shade, refuge, and water" of the Chirakari verse.

## Key Beats (in order)

1. **Bridge from Ch 4** ("This part, I choose."): Ferin could choose. Janvi "had to fight just to leave his house." "My life with him was hard. Hers was harder." The promise from Ch 4 is kept: "It belongs to Janvi."
2. **Birth and Lakshmi**: born 11 November 2002, about a week after Diwali. Ferin, not yet three, doesn't remember, but *Lakshmi aavi* is said in every Gujarati home. While she was small, the city reached Pal Gam (Ch 3 callback), and "the money came after she did" (indirect, per the plan). [Scripture: **Sri Sukta 2**: "bring me that Lakshmi who will never leave," for gold, cattle, horses, and men. The father treated his daughter the way the hymn treats the goddess: something that brings him things. "Ours left."] Hook: "This chapter is about the years in between."
3. **Shared childhood, no bond**: the same school and tuitions, the shared fear. No special father-daughter bond, "only fear." She saw what Ferin saw (the glass of water from Ch 2, her mother being hit). Brilliant and top of class. Easy to raise: calm, quiet, never asked for toys or clothes, lived her parents' dreams, never put her own choice first. Easy for her father especially, since he never had to do anything. "Her silence looked like contentment. It was fear." (The plan's "girls never demand" generalisation was kept personal to Janvi.)
4. **High school lectures**: the Ch 4 "monthly sessions" callback, with a different topic and the same idea. A girl won't smoke or drink, so: no stupid things, no relationships, only studies, the best marks. *"Tare doctor j banvanu chhe."* Image again: a better profile to show the Samaj. "It only got worse."
5. **Eleventh and twelfth grade**: science for both, maths for Ferin (engineering) and biology for Janvi (doctor). The same pressure, if not more on her. Nagging, no rest, a lecture if he saw her without a book. She cried. The plan's unworded line becomes: "A father is supposed to be the shade a child can rest under in those years. Janvi needed shade. Our father gave her more sun." [Scripture: **Kabir, the date-palm doha**: tall, no shade for the traveller, fruit out of reach. Tall in the Samaj, no shade at home, approval out of reach.] Ferin "learned to swallow it," while Janvi was gentler and cried (kept personal, not gendered). "I think he enjoys pressing down on people who cannot push back": his father, his wife, now his daughter. [Scripture: **Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 92.11**: strength was made for the sake of the weak, plus Utathya's warning (92.13–15) that the eye of the weak burns, "Never attack the weak." Home was the one place his word was final (Ch 4), so his strength was meant for her, and he used it on her instead.]
6. **Board exams and NEET 2020**: she cleared the boards with good grades. NEET facts: more than thirteen lakh appeared for about eighty-three thousand MBBS seats, about one in sixteen, with about half in government colleges where fees are far lower. COVID pushed the exam back twice (May → July → September). She was not allowed out, he called many times a day from work, and he reminded her of private-seat costs. "I know she gave it everything she had."
7. **The result**: a merit list, not pass or fail. She missed a general-category government seat by a few marks. Her heartbreak (her dream) against his fury (image): "Look at Shambu's daughter…" No other student her age in the Samaj had even sat NEET. [Scripture: **Gita 6.40**: to the one who tries and falls short, Krishna says no good effort ends in ruin, and calls Arjuna *tata* (the elder's tender word for a child). She needed one person to say it. Her father "did not say one gentle word to her."]
8. **"Take a drop."** (explained for non-Indian readers.) She offered another medical course instead. Reconstructed exchange: "Papa, I'll take another course…" / "And what will I tell people?" He forced and cursed her, cared only about his name, and she gave in. Ch 4 callback with honest chronology: "Three years later, I would give in to him the same way… I lost. Janvi lost first."
9. **The drop year (2020–21)**: Ferin saw what the father, at work every day but Sunday, did not: day and night study, friends cut off, no rest. He never noticed. Lectures went to a new level, calls doubled, and taunts grew. "Like a burden": "If you fail again, then what? What will I tell people then?" The plan's "Just imagine yourself…" becomes the narrator imagining it ("I have tried to imagine that year from inside her head"), because there is no reader address inside chapters. **Suicidal thoughts**, stated once and plainly: "More than once… she said she wanted to end her life." Then "Janvi wants this in the book. She wants it heard." Then the mother, whose care is reconstructed with the author's creative liberty: sitting up late, tea and food at the desk, holding her, sleeping beside her, "you have me." She did it while afraid of him herself. [Scripture: **Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 258.29** (Chirakari): no shade, refuge, protection, or water for the weary traveller like a mother. It also uses 258.27 (a mother protects whether the child is capable or not), and the angry father Gautama returns in tears, repentant.] "Kabir's date palm gave the traveller no shade. Janvi found her shade anyway." "My father came home from work every evening that year. He never once came home like that."
10. **Zydus, Dahod**: NEET in September 2021, a government-quota seat at Zydus Medical College, Dahod (a PPP with the Government of Gujarat), not Surat. Janvi happy, mother sad at the separation, father unhappy at losing control: "Away from me, with all that freedom, you will ruin your life." Ferin was in Halifax (from January 2022) and fought by phone, with the Ch 4 callback "the cage… rang." Janvi pushed back for the first time: Dahod or no medical school. The father pushed a second drop and a third attempt. She refused openly, ready to lose the dream but not another year. [Scripture: **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 3.8.2**: Gargi, silenced once (3.6.1, "or your head will fall off"), rises again "like a warrior stringing his bow," and Yajnavalkya answers "Ask, Gargi." Janvi was silenced once (the drop) and rose a second time.] Handoff: "My father did not say, 'Ask, Janvi.'"
11. **The raised hand**: "His answer was his hand." He raised it at his *own* daughter, the Lakshmi he showed off, a child who could take no more. "I was not there. I heard it afterwards…" "My mother stopped him." The Ch 3 callback ("Papa, please. Don't hit Mummy."): the woman he had hit for years stopped his hand. Then he cursed both. [Scripture: **Devi Mahatmya 11.6**: all knowledge and all women are forms of the Goddess. Janvi was "the Goddess twice over," a girl and a scholar.] "This time, my mother and I stood with Janvi… We accepted the seat at Zydus."
12. **She leaves**: "free of his tyranny at last," she packed at once. A girl usually leaves at marriage, in tears, but Janvi left at 19 and was glad. Not for freedom ("She had never asked for freedom") but for no pressure, and a little rest before medical school. "The Lakshmi of our house had left it." [Scripture: **Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 221.79**: Lakshmi tells Indra, "I will not live among the Danavas." Her reasons in the same speech: desire and anger (49), children not cared for (55), quarrels in every house (63). This closes the Sri Sukta frame: "Ours left for the same reasons."]
13. **Facebook and the forward thread**: "My daughter cracked NEET," on the same Facebook where the Samaj watched his passport fill (Ch 4 callback). "Hypocrisy, again." Other parents paid crores (private, management quota, other states), while she won a government-quota seat for a fraction of the cost. "It was hers." "She got there in spite of him." The closing forward thread from the plan: "something even bigger was waiting for her."
