## Chapter Summary

Chapter 5 ended on "something even bigger was waiting for her." Chapter 6 names it in its first line: marriage. It is still Janvi Patel's story, and it is the mirror of Chapter 4 (Ferin's marriage pressure), with heavier pressure.

While Janvi is deep in MBBS at Dahod (heavy course, a compulsory year of practice still ahead, hostel life, first time away from home), her father decides by the Samaj's clock that 23 is the age to get engaged. She refuses with reasons. He calls daily, shouts, and uses foul language. When she tells him it is affecting her studies and her mental health, he answers *"Mari samme aa badhu nai lavanu"* ("Don't bring all this in front of me. I don't care."). She stops coming home for festivals and holidays and stays alone in the hostel. She is still made to come for viewings, where he picks her dress and hair. His answers to her objections: "What is in the looks? After you have a child, what are you going to do with his looks?" and "You have to compromise." He uses Khushi as an example, and his brother Praful backs him. To "At least I am giving you a choice," Janvi replies that he is "falling for the balance sheet" of the boy's family: "I am not marrying his family. I am marrying him." He brings only doctors; she refuses them; "You have no options left."

The chapter then explains why options are few (the Samaj's land money without education). Janvi proposes her friend Jash. Her father first questions her character ("Are you having an affair with him?"), then rejects Jash: "He is a perfect boy. But his family does not have enough land." A counter-example father from the same society stands by his daughter. Then the father and Praful call Janvi an "earning cow." Finally: "I will marry you only where my heart wants." The chapter closes on why the book exists: he gets a clean start after every life he damages, the family's silence was taken as weakness, and the book sets the second column beside the first so readers can judge for themselves.

## Key Beats (in order)

1. **Bridge from Ch 5.** "What was waiting for her was marriage." Callback to Ch 4 (five biodatas, daily calls, one filter, "I lost"). The title set up as a name the father gave his daughter, to be explained later ("I will come to it").
2. **Dahod.** MBBS is heavy; a compulsory year of practice follows. Ferin calls and she is always studying. Hostel life is brutal: first time away, missing her mother. Her father looks at a calendar: Samaj girls marry at 24–25, so engagement at 23. Nobody asks if she is ready.
3. **Her reasons; the calls.** Not ready, no time for a new family or the boy, wants to finish MBBS, career. Deaf ears. Pressure heavier than on Ferin (a daughter must be "sent" on schedule). Daily calls, shouting, foul language. Callbacks: the cage that rang (Ch 4); Vidura on word-wounds (Ch 4).
4. **"I don't care."** "Papa, stop. This is affecting my studies. It is affecting my mental health." → *"Mari samme aa badhu nai lavanu."* [Verse: **Narsinh Mehta, "Vaishnav Jan To"** — a godly person knows another's pain.] He loses his place in her eyes; "Papa" holds nothing now (Ch 4 callback).
5. **The hostel instead of home.** She avoids festivals, long weekends, national holidays; stays alone in the hostel; would rather cry alone than go home. "The Lakshmi of our house had left it. Now she would not even visit." Ferin helpless from afar.
6. **Viewings.** Made to come home to see boys. He chooses her dress colour and hair. Women as objects (Ch 3 callback); Praful thinks the same.
7. **"What is in the looks?"** Expanded as the plan asks: marriage reduced to producing a child; a woman's liking given an expiry date; a marriage outlasts child-rearing (both his children left); he is describing the only marriage he knows; and looks did matter when she was the one being looked at.
8. **"You have to compromise."** Expanded: most parents exhaust themselves so a daughter need not settle; Janvi lacks nothing by the Samaj's own count; every item on her list was negotiable and the one on his (wealth) never was, so it is not a compromise; and the "profile" he built (*"Tare doctor j banvanu chhe"*) was for his story, not her choices.
9. **Khushi; Praful.** "Look at Khushi." One girl's choice is not a law; the circumstances differ. "Whatever I am doing is right." Praful pours oil on the fire, "the silent villain," but "my fight is not with my uncle."
10. **"At least I am giving you a choice."** Janvi's reply: one or two boys he likes; "the balance sheet of his family"; "I am not marrying his family. I am marrying him. What do you know about him?" No answer. Callback to Ferin's "Am I marrying her family, or the girl?" (Ch 4). Only doctors offered, though she does not want a doctor; some far older; "You have no options left." [Verse: **Bhagavata Purana 10.52.38** — Rukmini (Lakshmi) lists family, character, looks, learning, age, wealth, standing, and the girl chooses.]
11. **Why so few options.** The Samaj's land money came overnight; education did not; many dropped out and live on interest. Educated children have few matches (about 80% of girls for Ferin were not from science; a couple of doctor families for Janvi). Must she pay for the Samaj's choice? [Verse: **Chanakya Niti 8.19** — a great family without learning; a learned man of a low family. Paired with Ch 4's Chanakya 2.11, which credited the father for educating them.]
12. **Jash and land (*jameen*).** Janvi's friend of four or five years, same Samaj. "How dare you… Are you having an affair with him?" (the *lafda* callback, Ch 4). Her answer: computer engineer, same Canadian university as Ferin, well-behaved, mature, her age, decent well-known family. Days later: "He is a perfect boy. But his family does not have enough land." The land speech (no land money, nine-to-five, travel). Janvi: "That will be ours to worry about… I will be happy with him." [Verse: **Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva 44.2–3** — the duty that needs most thought: to what kind of man a daughter should be given; character, conduct, learning, family, work; "with water alone." No land on the list. 44.6 and 44.8 paraphrased: the wealth-decided marriage is the demons' way and must never be done.]
13. **Why land.** Expanded as the plan asks: the family rose on land, not on his salary (Ch 3), so land became the Samaj's measure. Ironies from established facts: he himself was the salaried, self-made man with little land (Ch 1), so "the boy he rejected was the boy he had once been"; he ran his house on his wife's father's land money (Ch 3); his own son lives abroad on a salary. Vidura 5.39.68 paraphrased. The real motive is image: "Look where Rakesh got his son engaged" (Ch 4), wanted a second time.
14. **The other father.** A daughter in their society engaged twice without success, then into a lower-middle-class family; her father told everyone, "I will take care of my daughter's future." "That is what I call a real father." Callback to Ashvapati (Ch 4).
15. **"An earning cow" (the title beat).** The father and Praful's term for Janvi; the sentence quoted. The section opens by pointing at the chapter title. Expanded as the plan asks: three insults (to Janvi, to Jash's family, and, unnoticed, to himself: "lost" implies ownership), and the contradiction with the land argument. Callback to the Sri Sukta's "gold, cattle" (Ch 5). [Verse: **Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva 45.24** — even a stranger must not be sold, how much less one's children. 45.21 (the pair of cows is still a sale), 45.19–20, and 45.12 (a daughter equals a son, a man's own self) paraphrased. The chapter states plainly that no money was changing hands.] "This is 2026."
16. **"Only where my heart wants."** "Do whatever you can. I will not allow anyone you bring to me." [Verse: **Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva 44.5** — the father sets aside his own wish and gives his daughter to the man she wishes for; counted among the righteous forms (44.9).] Callback to Gita 18.63 (Ch 4).
17. **Why this book.** No ending yet; Janvi, their mother, and Ferin are still fighting. He gets a clean start after each damaged life (grandfather, mother, Ferin, Janvi, and others). The family wiped the account themselves by staying silent; he took silence for weakness (Vidura callback, Ch 3). "Enough is enough." The book as the second time Ferin stands up (Vikarna callback, Ch 3). Not to persuade, but to show the whole man. [Verse: **Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.1** — *satyam vada*; the title verse's own teaching begins with "Speak the truth."] Both columns of the balance sheet; "Whoever reads it is free to do the sum." Title answered: "My sister is nobody's earning cow. She is still fighting for the right to choose her own life." Last lines: "But the silence is over. That part, at least, is finished."
