## Notes

- All Chapter 2 verses (4) and Chapter 3 verses (4 at drafting, plus 1 added in the 2026-09-28 revision) were checked against external sources (2026-08-20, 2026-08-27, and 2026-09-28) rather than drawn purely from model memory, per `CONTEXT.md`'s "Accuracy is the book's armor" rule.
- **Edition lesson (2026-09-28)**: a verse's best-known wording can belong to a different edition, or a different text, than the one being cited. The "where women are honoured, the gods rejoice" line is missing from the Mahabharata Critical Edition, and its most famous form is Manusmriti 3.56. For Mahabharata verses, check the Critical Edition e-text first. The sanskritdocuments.org mirror (`https://sanskritdocuments.org/mirrors/mahabharata/txt/mbhNN.itx`, NN = parva number) can be downloaded and searched with `grep`, which works even when wisdomlib, sacred-texts, and vedapath block fetching.
- **Rigveda 10.85.46 analysis revised (2026-09-28)**: "He had stood through that exact ceremony" became "He had stood at that fire himself." We cannot confirm this particular verse was recited at Rakesh and Heena Patel's own wedding. The analysis also now says the two brides of this joint family "were made to serve," tying it to the chapter's new opening section.
- The Ramayana verse's Devanagari (Ch 2) was reconstructed from a confirmed romanized transliteration rather than fetched directly in Devanagari — worth a second look before publication, though the wording is standard and unambiguous.
- **No verse has been reused across chapters.** Chapter 1 deliberately held all scripture back. Chapter 2 uses Chanakya Niti 2.5, Bhagavad Gita 2.62–63, Mahabharata Vana Parva (Yaksha Prashna), Valmiki Ramayana Ayodhya Kanda 24.1. Chapter 3 uses, in order of appearance, Mahabharata Anushasana Parva 46.5 (added 2026-09-28), Bhagavad Gita 16.21, Rigveda 10.85.46, Mahabharata Udyoga Parva 93.48, Bhagavad Gita 3.6. The Gita is drawn on twice in this chapter (16.21 and 3.6) and so is the Mahabharata (Anushasana, Udyoga). Each pair does distinct work (the vices named / the word for hypocrisy; the honour owed to the women of a house / the guilt of those who watch), which is acceptable given the chapter's themes map directly onto all of them.
- **Source spread so far**: Bhagavad Gita ×3 (2.62–63, 16.21, 3.6), Mahabharata ×3 (Vana Parva, Udyoga Parva, Anushasana Parva), Chanakya Niti ×1, Valmiki Ramayana ×1, Rigveda ×1. The Vedas beyond the Rigveda, and the Arthashastra, are still unused — candidates for later chapters.
- **Open pre-publication check**: the Mahabharata Udyoga Parva 93.48 citation's chapter/verse numbering follows one structured Sanskrit source and may vary by recension (Gita Press vs. Critical Edition) — verify against a print edition before finalising, and consider citing by parva + episode only (as done for the Yaksha Prashna verse) if the number proves edition-dependent.
- **Chapter 4 (2026-10-11)** uses 12 verses from 7 distinct texts (counting the Gita separately from the rest of the Mahabharata), at the author's request for "more scripture... from more books than I listed at first." In order: Chanakya Niti 2.11, Gita 6.17, Gita 12.15, Chanakya Niti 3.18, Kabir (Sakhi), Gita 18.63, Yoga Vasishtha 2.18.3–4, Mahabharata Udyoga Parva 34.75 (Vidura Niti), Gita 3.21, Katha Upanishad 1.1.4, Mahabharata Aranyaka Parva 278.26 (Savitri), Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2. **New sources:** Yoga Vasishtha, Katha Upanishad, Taittiriya Upanishad, Sant Kabir. No verse repeats one from Ch 2–3. The Gita appears four times in Ch 4, each with distinct work (balance, fear, choice, setting a standard).
- **Method that worked (2026-10-11)**: Mahabharata and Gita verses were checked in the BORI Critical Edition e-texts (`mbh03.itx`, `mbh05.itx`, `mbh06.itx` from the sanskritdocuments.org mirror; the Gita is MBh 6.23–6.40, so Gita ch N = MBh 6.(22+N)). Chanakya Niti and the Upanishads were checked against Sanskrit Wikisource using `?action=raw` on the page URL, which returns plain wikitext and is not blocked. The sanskritdocuments `doc_upanishhat/*.itx` URLs returned 404.
- **Source spread after Ch 4**: Bhagavad Gita ×7, Mahabharata (non-Gita) ×5, Chanakya Niti ×3, Valmiki Ramayana ×1, Rigveda ×1, Upanishads ×2, Yoga Vasishtha ×1, Kabir ×1.
