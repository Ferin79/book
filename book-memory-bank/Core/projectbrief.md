**High-Level Concept**: An unflinching account of the gap between Rakesh Patel's carefully maintained public image — modern, devoutly Hindu, caring — and his private conduct as a father and husband, told through specific real incidents and measured against classical Hindu scripture (Bhagavad Gita, Mahabharata, Valmiki Ramayana, Chanakya Niti, Vedas).

**Premise**: Rakesh Patel is, in the eyes of society, the model of a modern, devout, caring Hindu family man. In the eyes of his children, he consistently placed his personal pride and social standing above his duties as a husband and father. The book argues that this kind of hidden harm — virtue performed in public, absent in private — is more damaging to a family and to society than open, undisguised failure, because the performance is what earns trust and shields the harm from scrutiny.

## Core Elements

**Central Real-Life Circumstance**: A father whose public devotion to modernity, Hindu faith, and community care conceals a private record of neglecting and harming his own wife and children.

**Key Themes**:
1. The gap between public performance of virtue and private conduct
2. Dharma/duty as actually defined by scripture vs. dharma as performed for social credit
3. The specific, compounding harm of a father who prioritizes pride/reputation over his family

**Father's Real Goal(s)**: Maintaining social standing and reputation as a modern, devout, respected family man.

**What Was Genuinely at Stake**: The wellbeing of his wife and children, subordinated (per their account) to his public image.

## Project Goals

**Purpose**: To document the true gap between Rakesh Patel's public image and private conduct, for the author and his sister's own reckoning — and, beyond that, to give readers living under a similar authoritative, image-conscious figure a model for recognizing the pattern, a scripturally grounded vocabulary for what real dharma/righteousness looks like versus its performance, and encouragement to raise their voice against it.

**Reader Experience Goal**: Recognition and validation for readers who see their own household in this story; a restored, clear-eyed understanding of dharma, freedom, and duty as they're actually defined, not as they're performed for social credit; and the sense that speaking the truth about this kind of harm is possible and worthwhile.

**Publication Goal**: Undecided (traditional publishing / self-publishing / undecided) — the author intends for this to eventually reach readers beyond the family, so factual rigor and legal considerations should be treated as if publication is likely, even while the repository stays private during drafting.
