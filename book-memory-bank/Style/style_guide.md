# Style Guide

This document defines the writing style, language choices, and formatting conventions for this book. Confirmed with the author starting Chapter 1 (2026-08-17). It records project-specific *decisions*; the full rule set behind these decisions lives in `.claude/skills/book-writer/references/author_rules.md` and `references/narrative_nonfiction_rules.md` — read this file for the confirmed choices, those for the complete mechanics.

## Voice & Tone

**Narrative Voice**: Warm, observational, literary but grounded — the voice of someone who loved and studied this person closely, not a detached reporter or a eulogy. Confirmed with the author over the fiction-workshop alternatives ("more conversational" and "more formal/journalistic") — this is the established default for every chapter unless a specific chapter calls for a deliberate shift (e.g., a colder register for a scene of real distance).

**Tone**: Emotionally honest, never melodramatic or sentimental for its own sake. Restraint over drama — the facts, told specifically, carry the weight. No hagiography, no character assassination.

**Emotional Range**: Full range as the real story requires (warmth, pride, disappointment, quiet contentment, and — in later chapters — real hurt and anger), but always earned through concrete detail rather than asserted directly to the reader.

**Reader Relationship**: The narrator does not address the reader directly inside chapters (no second-person "you," no direct commentary on the book's wider purpose). Direct reader address is reserved for the bookend material only — a possible Author's Note (front matter) and Afterword (back matter) — per `CONTEXT.md` → "Reconciling the Personal and the Universal."

## Point of View

**Primary POV**: First person. Ferin Patel (the author) is the narrator throughout the book. The father, Rakesh Patel, and other family members are written in third person within Ferin's first-person frame (standard memoir structure — "I" narrating, "he"/"my father" as subject).

**POV Shifts**: None within a chapter. If his sister Janvi Patel's perspective is incorporated, it is clearly attributed as her account, not folded into Ferin's own memory.

**Interior Thoughts**: Woven directly into the narrative prose, not italicized.

**Knowledge Limitations**: The narrator only knows what he was told or witnessed. Where the record is genuinely incomplete (e.g., the specifics of how Rakesh Patel financed his own education), that gap is treated as real narrative material — acknowledged in prose — rather than papered over with invented specifics.

## Language Choices

**Vocabulary Level**: Plain, common words over rare/formal ones (*walked* not *traversed*). One idea per sentence.

**Sentence Structure**: Varied — short, direct sentences for action/movement; longer, unhurried sentences for reflection. Never convoluted.

**Paragraph Length**: Short to medium (roughly 3–5 lines); varied for rhythm, not walls of text.

**Dialect and Speech Patterns**: Gujarati words/phrases are woven into the English narration where that's how it was actually said (e.g., *fadiyu*, *fali*, greetings), introduced with enough surrounding context that the reader infers meaning. No bracketed translations.

## Dialogue

**Dialogue Tags**: Minimal, plain ("said" over decorative verbs) when used at all.

**Action Beats**: Break up dialogue with small physical gestures rather than adverb-heavy tags.

**Internal Dialogue**: Not italicized; woven into prose.

**Dialect and Accents**: Represented through natural word choice and rhythm, not phonetic spelling.

## Grammar & Mechanics

**Tense**: Past tense for all narrated events and biography. Present tense reserved for the narrator stepping back to name a lasting pattern or general truth (e.g., "That habit never left him.").

**Punctuation Preferences**: Oxford comma always. Em dashes sparingly (interruption/sharp shifts only). Semicolons sparingly. Ellipses only for genuine trailing off.

**Capitalization**: Standard; proper names and place names as given.

**Numbers**: Prose spells out counts/durations/ages under ten; numerals for ages ten and up. Dates and years are always numerals. See `author_rules.md` §2 for the full table.

## Formatting

**Chapter Headers**: `## Chapter [Number]: [Title]`, with a `# Part [Roman Numeral] — [Part Name]` heading above the first chapter of a new part.

**Scene Breaks**: Exactly three dashes (`---`), centered on their own line.

**Emphasis**: Italics for foreign-language words/phrases, titles of works, and inner emphasis. No bold text anywhere in narrative prose.

**Special Text**: Primary-source material (letters, documents) as Markdown blockquotes. Scripture references also use blockquotes, per the format fixed in `CONTEXT.md` → "The Scripture & Reference Framework."

## Genre-Specific Conventions

**Setting Descriptions**: Delivered through specific, physical, sensory detail woven into action — never a standalone descriptive info-dump.

**Emotional Scenes**: Restraint. Let silence, subtext, and small gestures carry the weight; what people don't say matters more than what they do.

**Exposition / Biographical Weaving**: Never dump pages of static biography. Introduce a person's history or circumstances at the moment they become relevant to what's happening in the scene (per `narrative_nonfiction_rules.md` §4).

**Chapter Openings (per-chapter default)**: Scene-first — open on a concrete, lived image or moment, then ease into biography/context, rather than opening with a flat factual statement. Confirmed with the author for Chapter 1; carry forward as the default unless a specific chapter's material calls for a different opening formula (see `references/chapter_craft.md` → "Chapter Opening Formulas").

**Scripture Placement (per-chapter decision, not automatic)**: The scripture-reference framework in `CONTEXT.md` is load-bearing for the book overall, but is not mechanically inserted into every chapter. For Chapter 1 — a "portrait" chapter establishing the public image before it's complicated — the author chose to hold scripture back and keep the chapter purely biographical/scene-grounded. Introduce scripture starting from whichever chapter first shows the gap between his public image and private conduct, or wherever a specific incident calls for the counterpoint hardest. Re-confirm per chapter rather than assuming.

## Character-Specific Elements

**Name Usage**: First introduction uses the full real name to ground identity (e.g., "Rakesh Patel"). Subsequent references use first name, relation term ("my father"), or — specifically for Rakesh Patel — his neighborhood nickname "Shambu" when the narration is inside that community context. No invented pet names not confirmed by the author.

## Problem Areas to Watch

**Info Dumps**: The single biggest risk in early chapters given how much biographical fact exists per person — always weave, never list.

**Invented Specificity**: Never fill a genuine factual gap (an unknown date, an unremembered detail) with a plausible-sounding invented one — flag it in prose or with `[NEED RESEARCH]` instead.

## Notes

The version of "write like a human author" that applies to this project is the memoir voice above — warm, restrained, concrete — not a fiction-workshop voice. Contractions and informal rhythm are welcome in narration and especially in remembered dialogue; avoid perfectly modulated speeches. Use similes/metaphors sparingly and only when they're grounded in this family's actual world (Gujarat, Surat, the lane, the mandir) rather than generic comparisons.
