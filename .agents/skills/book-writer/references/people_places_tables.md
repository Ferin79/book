# People & Places Tables — Structured Profile Templates

Use these structured table templates when documenting real people, real places, real conflicts, the synopsis, and the timeline. These templates integrate into the Book Memory Bank — fill them in as facts are gathered and store them in `book-memory-bank/Core/people_and_places.md` (or the relevant Core file).

Every field here describes something real. Where a fact is unknown, write `[TBD]` or `[NEED RESEARCH]` — never invent a plausible-sounding answer.

---

## Person Profile Table

Create one table per person who matters to the story. Store in `book-memory-bank/Core/people_and_places.md`.

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **NAME** | Full real name |
| **RELATIONSHIP** | Father / Mother / Sibling / Spouse / Friend / Colleague / etc. |
| **PRONOUNS** | He/him, she/her, they/them, etc. |
| **OTHER NAMES** | Nicknames, what family called them, any name changes |
| **LIFE DATES** | Birth year – death year (or – present) |
| **PERSONALITY** | 3–5 core real traits |
| **WANTS / DROVE THEM** | What they visibly pursued or valued, in life and within the story's timeframe |
| **INNER STRUGGLE** | A real, honest internal tension — memoir doesn't flatten people into heroes or villains |
| **STRENGTHS** | Real skills, virtues, resources |
| **STRUGGLES / FLAWS** | Real weaknesses, hardships, blind spots |
| **HOW THEY'RE SHOWN TO CHANGE** | How the book's portrayal of them deepens or shifts across chapters |
| **KEY RELATIONSHIPS** | Connections to other real people in the book |
| **SHARED HISTORY** | Background connecting them to other people in the story |
| **ROLE IN THE BOOK** | Their function in the narrative (what chapters/events center on them) |
| **BACKGROUND ANCHOR** | One real, formative experience that shaped who they were |
| **NOTABLE DETAILS** | Real quirks, appearance, speech patterns, mannerisms |
| **SPEECH VOICE** | How they actually talked — cadence, vocabulary, dialect, verbal tics |
| **TITLE / ROLE TIMELINE** | Each real title/role and the date/event that conferred it |
| **CURRENT ADDRESS** | Correct form of address at the narrative's current point in time |
| **RECONSTRUCTED ELEMENTS** | Any scenes/dialogue involving them that are reconstructed from memory rather than directly documented |

## Place & Setting Table

One table per significant real place. Store in `book-memory-bank/Core/people_and_places.md`.

| Category | Details |
|----------|---------|

| **TIME PERIOD RELEVANT** | When in the story this place matters |

## Conflict & Struggle Mapping Table

Map the real tensions and struggles in the story — these come from real life, not invented antagonists.

### External Circumstances
| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **THE CIRCUMSTANCE** | What was actually happening (illness, poverty, war, displacement, family conflict, institutional barriers, etc.) |
| **WHO/WHAT IT CAME FROM** | The real source of the difficulty — a person, an institution, an event, circumstance |
| **THE STAKES** | What was genuinely at risk |

### Internal Struggle
| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **THE STRUGGLE** | The person's real inner tension, as best understood |
| **THE FEAR** | What they were afraid of, as far as can be known or reasonably inferred |
| **THE GROWTH OR COST** | What they learned, sacrificed, or never resolved |

### Thematic Thread
| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **THE QUESTION** | The central question this part of the real story raises for the reader |
| **THE TENSION** | Two real, competing values or pressures in the person's life |
| **HOW IT LANDED** | How this actually resolved (or didn't) in real life — memoirs are allowed to leave things unresolved, because life often does |

## Synopsis Template

Use this structure to outline the full arc of the real story. Store as part of `story_structure.md` or the master outline.

### Beginning (Setup)
> 2–3 paragraphs covering:
> - Where the story starts and the family's situation at that point
> - Introduction of the father and key people
> - The real event that sets the book's main story in motion
> - What was genuinely at stake

### Middle (Life Unfolding)
> 3–4 paragraphs covering:
> - Real complications and how circumstances developed
> - How relationships deepened or changed
> - Real turning points — 1–2 major shifts that changed the family's direction
> - Emotional escalation as it actually happened

### End (Where It Lands)
> 2–3 paragraphs covering:
> - The most significant real moment(s) toward the end of the book's scope
> - How things actually resolved (or where the story chooses to end)
> - What the book ultimately says, in the author's own understanding
> - The emotional note the reader should be left with

## How to Use These Tables

1. **During initialization (Story Forge)** — The AI proposes pre-filled tables based on the author's answers and asks for confirmation, never invents facts to fill gaps.
2. **During conversation** — Build out people and places iteratively as the author shares more; store confirmed details in these table formats.
3. **During writing** — The AI reads these tables to maintain factual and voice consistency across chapters.
4. **During memory bank updates** — New details discovered while drafting get added to the appropriate table fields.
5. **During continuity checks** — These tables serve as the reference standard for flagging factual or continuity inconsistencies.
